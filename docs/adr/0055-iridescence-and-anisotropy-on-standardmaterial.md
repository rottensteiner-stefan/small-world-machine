# 0055: Anisotropy and iridescence on `StandardMaterial` — the last two lanes, a tiered iridescence model, textures as a later step

> **Status note 2026-11-01 (no decision change):** the WebGL2 texture step of this ADR (`anisotropyTexture`, `iridescenceTexture`, `iridescenceThicknessTexture`) was implemented and GPU-verified in the same working session on 2026-10-31 (see `Textures` below); the lingering backlog entry about it was closed as done on 2026-11-01. The ADR-0054 revisit of 2026-11-01 restructured the WebGPU material bindings into per-flag groups with a budget fit; the anisotropy/iridescence textures deliberately stay WebGL2-only and are registered as the documented follow-up there. The phrase "WebGPU keeps its 15 of 16 texture budget" below describes the pre-revisit state; the decision it belongs to (WebGL2-only sampling, factors elsewhere) is unchanged.

- **Status:** Accepted (human-approved 2026-10-31)
- **Date:** 2026-10-31
- **Trigger:** Showcase 13 glTF audit listed `KHR_materials_anisotropy` and `KHR_materials_iridescence` as missing (after `KHR_materials_specular`, ADR 0054).

## Decision

- **Anisotropy** (implemented): `StandardMaterial.anisotropyStrength` / `anisotropyRotation` travel in lane `u_matParam15` = `(strength, cos(rotation), sin(rotation), 0)` on all tiers. BRDF is the Khronos non-normative one: Burley anisotropic GGX `D` times the height-correlated visibility `V` (`specularAnisotropic`, which carries the `1 / (4 NL NV)` term, so callers multiply by `F`), `alpha_t = mix(alpha, 1, strength^2)`, `alpha_b = alpha`. The IBL specular lookup uses the Khronos single-sample bent normal (`anisotropyBentNormal`); the clearcoat keeps the plain reflection vector. `T` is the rotated tangent direction, `B = cross(geometric normal, T)`. Strength 0 is off: every tier compiles the path only behind `USE_ANISOTROPY` (see compile-time gating below; the Decal program, which shares the chunk, stays untouched).
- **Iridescence** (implemented, factors): `iridescenceFactor`, `iridescenceIor`, `iridescenceThicknessMinimum/Maximum` in lane `u_matParam14` = `(factor, ior, thicknessMin, thicknessMax)`; factor 0 is off (every tier compiles the path only behind `USE_IRIDESCENCE`). Model per tier, as the Khronos spec allows approximations by device performance: **WebGPU and WebGL2** use the Belcour & Barla evaluation line for line as in `iridescence.glsl` of the Khronos sample renderer (spectral sensitivity in Fourier space, reflection orders m = 0..2; constants, matrix and structure were diffed against the original); **WebGL1** uses a cheap two-beam RGB film model (Fresnel at both interfaces, Snell refraction, interference at three fixed wavelengths, the idea of the oil materials in `liquid-extras`, whose code the engine may not import), parametrised by film IOR and thickness. The performance-tier switch for WebGPU/WebGL2 is `QualityConfig.cheapIridescence` (default `false`; the `LOW` tier of ADR 0035 sets it to `true`, an explicit author value wins in both directions): the renderers (`resolveManifestFlags`) add the renderer-injected flag `USE_IRIDESCENCE_CHEAP` to iridescent programs only, and the WGSL / GLSL 300 chunks then compile `iridescenceFresnelCheap` (the WebGL1 two-beam model, ported) instead of `evalIridescence` and its spectral helpers. WebGL1 ignores the setting (it always runs the cheap model). The cheap model keeps the tint but is not the spectral look: on the probe sphere (dielectric, 400 nm film) up to 94 (WebGL2) / 125 (WebGPU) of 255 per channel differ from the spectral render, mostly a hue shift of the body and a greenish rim. To keep the cost off the per-light loops the thin-film Fresnel is evaluated **once per fragment at the view angle** with the combined base F0 (dielectric/metal mix, one call instead of two) and folded back into `F0` through `schlickToF0`, so every existing Fresnel call picks it up; the spec's per-light `VdotH` evaluation is approximated this way (the spec leaves the BRDF choice to the device, three.js takes the same shortcut). Without a thickness texture the film has the maximum thickness, as the spec says.
- **Lane budget:** `u_matParam14` and `u_matParam15` were the last free lanes on `StandardMaterial` (12/13 are specular, ADR 0054). After both features the 16-lane bank is full; any further per-material feature needs a bank growth, i.e. its own ADR (the UBO size changes for every material and tier).
- **Textures** (implemented as a second step): `anisotropyTexture` (RG direction in tangent space, rotated by `anisotropyRotation`; B multiplies the strength), `iridescenceTexture` (R multiplies the factor) and `iridescenceThicknessTexture` (G mixes the thickness range) are sampled on **WebGL2 only**, behind `USE_ANISOTROPY_MAP`, `USE_IRIDESCENCE_MAP`, `USE_IRIDESCENCE_THICKNESS_MAP` (ADR 0054 policy: WebGPU keeps its 15 of 16 texture budget, WebGL1 ignores them). WebGPU and WebGL1 use the factors.
- **WebGL2 sampler budget (measured, `GL2SamplerBudget.test.ts`):** units 11–15 are reserved by the renderer, so every other sampler (diffuse, normal, material maps, IBL, EVSM) shares units 0–10: **11 free units**, and a program that needs more used to throw in `WebGLProgramCache._assertTextureUnits` (the material was not drawn; ADR 0056 now drops extension maps instead). A glTF PBR material (diffuse, normal, metallic, roughness, AO, emissive, 3 IBL samplers) already takes **9**. Two measures keep the new textures usable: (1) when `metallicMap` and `roughnessMap` are the same texture object (the glTF metallicRoughness layout) `USE_METALLIC_ROUGHNESS_SHARED` reads both channels through the one `u_metallicMap` sampler (saves one unit for every glTF PBR material on WebGL2); (2) when the iridescence and thickness textures are the same texture (the usual packed layout; the glTF loader reuses one texture for equal texture indices) `USE_IRIDESCENCE_MAPS_SHARED` reads the thickness from the green channel of `u_iridescenceMap`. With both, a helmet-class material plus EVSM, anisotropy texture and packed iridescence textures fits (11 of 11). It does **not** fit when specular maps (2, ADR 0054) and separate iridescence textures come on top (13): the resolver of ADR 0056 drops the iridescence maps there, and the test documents the case.
- **Compile-time gating on every tier:** the anisotropy and iridescence code (helper functions, setup, call sites) sits in `#ifdef USE_ANISOTROPY` / `USE_IRIDESCENCE` blocks, so a material without the feature compiles the same shader as before the features existed. WebGL1 and WebGL2 get this from the `#define` each flag becomes; WGSL has no preprocessor, so `GPUPipelineCache` runs the new `preprocessConditionals` (`#ifdef` / `#ifndef` / `#else` / `#endif`, nesting) over the assembled source before compiling. This replaced the first, runtime-gated WGSL/WebGL1 implementation (output of all tiers verified pixel-identical before and after). The first-frame time of a plain PBR material was measured before and after on SwiftShader (WebGPU, WebGL2, WebGL1) and shows no difference beyond noise either way, so the gating is justified by shader size and register pressure (a feature-less material runs exactly the pre-feature shader), not by a measured compile-time gain.

## Rejected alternatives (kept for later)

- **Iridescence: cheap two-beam model on every tier.** Less code, but visibly off the reference look on the tiers that can afford the real thing.
- **Iridescence: spec-faithful Belcour & Barla on WebGL1.** Instruction limit and precision risk on weak hardware for a feature that needs no API capability the lower tiers lack.
- **Anisotropy packed into lane 13 `yzw`.** Saves a lane but breaks one semantic per lane (ADR 0027/0039), the contract that keeps material and shader from silently detaching.
- **Growing the lane bank beyond 16.** Changes the UBO size for all materials and all three tiers: large intervention with golden risk, not justified by two features.
- **Factors and textures in one step**, or **no textures at all:** the first mixes a large risk surface into the BRDF commits, the second leaves the typical anisotropy asset (brushed metal with a direction map) without its texture.

## Revisit when

- A further per-material feature needs a lane: grow the bank (own ADR) instead of packing.
- A real asset needs a different map priority than the global one of the sampler-budget resolver (ADR 0056, which replaced the fail-fast throw); also revisit ADR 0054's WebGPU texture-budget decision.
- Anisotropic IBL needs more than one sample (Khronos mentions extra taps along `T` for very shiny bases).

## Doctrine Compliance

- **New public hot-scope parameters (classification):** `StandardMaterial.anisotropyStrength` and `anisotropyRotation` are `@domain` (glTF authoring values, defaults 0). Lane contents `u_matParam15` (cos/sin folded on the CPU) are `@derived`.
- **Shared abstraction >= 3 consumers?** `specularAnisotropic` / `anisotropyBentNormal` have one semantic consumer (anisotropy) and are tier-local math helpers next to `F_Schlick`; they reuse the typed-lane mechanism (ADR 0027 / 0039) instead of adding one.
- **Modeful object?** The enable state has a single basis (`anisotropyStrength > 0`) for the `USE_ANISOTROPY` compile flag (the lane value follows from the same strength). Transition test: returning to strength 0 re-disables lane and flag (`MaterialAnisotropy.test.ts`).
- **Mathematical claims:** the anisotropic `D` is a normalised distribution and equals the engine's isotropic GGX when `alpha_t = alpha_b`; both are checked numerically in `MaterialAnisotropy.test.ts` (hemisphere integral, identity case). The thin-film evaluation is checked in `MaterialIridescence.test.ts` against a mirror of the shader math (finite and non-negative over the parameter range, plain Schlick at normal incidence for a vanishing film, thickness-dependent colour shift, `schlickToF0` inverts Schlick); shader constants are pinned against the Khronos values. No registry invariant claimed.
- **Fail-closed / honest fallback:** not render-critical; the anisotropy texture is not read yet and the limit is documented here and in the loader.
- **Exceptions:** none.

## Consequences

- On WebGPU and WebGL1, assets with `anisotropyTexture`, `iridescenceTexture` or `iridescenceThicknessTexture` render with the factors only (a uniform direction / full-strength film at maximum thickness instead of per-texel variation); WebGL2 samples them. Verified on the GPU with procedural textures: a +y direction texture turns the WebGL2 highlight vertical while WebGPU keeps the factor result, and WebGPU renders pixel-identically with and without the iridescence textures.
- Iridescence on WebGPU/WebGL2 costs one `evalIridescence` per fragment (two spectral sensitivity evaluations) when the factor is above 0; materials without the feature pay nothing (compile-time gate on every tier).
- WebGL2 programs over the device's texture-unit budget no longer throw: the sampler-budget resolver (ADR 0056) drops the lowest-priority extension maps and warns; the shared metallic/roughness sampler still gives every glTF PBR material one unit of headroom, so fewer materials reach the resolver at all.
- The Khronos reference evaluates the top interface as `Schlick(R0 = 0, cosTheta1)`, which keeps grazing-angle reflectance even for a vanishing film; the shaders reproduce it faithfully, so a film of thickness ~0 equals plain Schlick only near normal incidence.
- `RainPuddleMaterial` (`AbstractMaterial`) uses lane numbers 12/13 for its own shader and is unaffected; `EmberSpriteMaterial` (extends `StandardMaterial`) uses none of 12–15.
